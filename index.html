About

Data-driven, AI-native, commercial leader.

I started my career at Google and Meta in Dublin, advising some of Europe's largest advertisers on measurement, attribution and experimentation. That foundation shapes how I lead: marketing is an investment, and it should be measured like one.

Since then I have led marketing across banking, payments, EdTech and fintech. I drove the digital transformation of group marketing across 13 CEE markets, built a performance organisation during hypergrowth, and as VP led a 34-person global team across 40+ markets, supporting $550M+ in M&A along the way.

Today, AI is at the core of how I run marketing. I have built an AI-powered sales offers workflow tool for our sales team, used AI to scale content production across channels without adding headcount, and built AI-assisted dashboards that give leadership real-time insight. Outside work, I use AI to build my own websites and apps, which today reach more than 350,000 users every month. For me, AI is not a side project: it is how a modern marketing organisation does more, faster, with sharper decisions.

I sit at the leadership table, work closely with sales and product, and report to the board. I care about building teams that perform without me in the room.

How I transform marketing

From audit to growth engine in 12 months.

A clear, proven sequence. Adapted to each company, but always measured.

  1. 01 · First 30 days

    Listen & diagnose

    Audit the funnel, data, budget, team and agencies. Meet customers, sales and product. Find where marketing leaks revenue.

  2. 02 · Days 30–90

    Align & deliver quick wins

    Agree a growth plan, KPIs and budget with the CEO and sales. Fix tracking, cut wasted spend and ship the first wins.

  3. 03 · Months 3–6

    Build the engine

    Set up measurement and reporting the board trusts, structure the team, and build AI into content, sales enablement and analytics.

  4. 04 · Months 6–12

    Scale & prove

    Double down on what works across channels and markets. Marketing is reported as a revenue driver, not a cost centre.

The outcome: a marketing organisation the board sees as a measurable investment, powered by data and AI, and a team that keeps performing without me in the room.
